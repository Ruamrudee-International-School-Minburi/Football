Play the game. Then play it better.

A plain-English guide to football (soccer): how a match works, what each position does, and the drills that make you improve fastest.

Start with the pitch

Know where everyone plays

Eleven players per side, one goalkeeper and ten outfield. Tap a player on this 4-3-3 to see what the role asks of you.

The rules that matter most

You can start playing with these six.

The goal

Score more than the other team. The whole ball must cross the line between the posts and under the bar.

Match length

Two halves of 45 minutes in the adult game. Youth and casual games are often shorter.

Hands

Only the goalkeeper, inside their own box, may handle the ball. Everyone else uses feet, body and head.

Offside

When a teammate passes, you can’t be nearer the goal line than the ball and the second-last defender, unless you’re in your own half.

Fouls

Tripping, pushing or holding gives a free kick. A foul inside the defender’s box gives a penalty.

Restarts

Throw-in when it leaves the side, goal kick or corner when it leaves the end, depending on who touched it last.

Five skills, five drills

Short, focused practice beats long, tired practice. Pick a skill and try its drill.

Habits that make you better in matches

Scan before the ball arrives. Look over your shoulder two or three times. Players who know where space is play faster.

Use both feet. Spend a third of every drill on your weaker foot. It becomes a real weapon within weeks.

Move after you pass. Passing and standing still blocks your team. Pass, then run into a new space.

Keep it simple under pressure. A short, safe pass is better than a risky dribble in your own half.

Talk constantly. Call for the ball, warn teammates about opponents behind them, and shout “time” when they’re free.

Watch good players. Follow one player in your position for a whole match and note where they run without the ball.

Recover well. Drink water, eat protein and carbohydrates after sessions, and sleep eight hours or more.

Play lots of small games. Three-a-side gives you many more touches than a full pitch.

Pitch Notes is a general guide. If you’re returning from injury or training hard, check with a coach or doctor first.
